## Course page

**Learn how a Chinese festival of weaving and skill can inspire respectful, contemporary fashion design.**

Qixi, the Double Seventh Festival, is best known today as a celebration of love, yet its traditions are rooted in weaving, needlework and skill. This free reading course is based on the Fibre2Fashion article “Qixi and Fashion: Ancient Chinese Heritage Meets Modern Design” by Kshipra Kulkarni. It introduces the Weaver Girl legend and the qiqiao tradition, then shows how designers can translate the story into woven structures, embroidery, garment construction, textile surfaces and colour without copying traditional imagery. It also covers luxury fashion communication, the shift from romance to self-expression, opportunities for Indian fashion, and the research and cultural sensitivity needed for a respectful design brief.

**Who it is for:** Fashion design and textile students, designers, merchandisers and brand teams who want to draw on cultural festivals thoughtfully in their collections.

**You will learn to:**
- Explain the Qixi legend, the qiqiao tradition and their textile roots.
- Translate festival symbols into weaves, embroidery, construction and contemporary silhouettes.
- Use colour and communication to tell a craft-led cultural story.
- Adapt a cultural narrative while keeping your own design identity.
- Research a cultural festival and turn it into a respectful design brief.

**Source:** this course is based on the Fibre2Fashion article [Qixi and Fashion: Ancient Chinese Heritage Meets Modern Design](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/industry-article/11939/qixi-festival-fashion-ancient-chinese-heritage-modern-design) by Kshipra Kulkarni (Nov 2026).
