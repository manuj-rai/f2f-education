# Welcome to Staple Yarn Spinning
Every cotton T-shirt, wool jumper and linen shirt starts as a pile of short fibres. This one-week online module shows you how those fibres become yarn, and why the choices made in the spinning mill decide how a fabric looks, feels, performs and costs. No engineering background is needed.

## Who this course is for
Merchandisers, buyers and sourcing teams, mill sales and technical staff, and graduate entrants to the textile and apparel industry. If you specify, buy or sell yarn or fabric, this course gives you the vocabulary and the questions to ask.

## What you will be able to do
- Explain the difference between staple and filament yarn, and between carded, semi-combed and combed yarn.
- Describe in plain terms how ring, rotor, compact and air-jet spinning work, and when each is used.
- Read a yarn specification (count, twist, ply, fibre, spinning route) and predict how the fabric will behave.
- Identify the main cost drivers at yarn level and the quality trade-offs of cheaper options.
- Ask informed questions about fibre origin, certification and labour conditions when you assess a sustainability claim.

## How the course works
The module has four units and an applied assignment. Work through them in order: each unit builds on the one before.

| Unit | What you will do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1. What is a staple yarn? | Short video, reading, knowledge check | 45 min |
| 2. How spinning works | Reading, process-order exercise, knowledge check | 60 min |
| 3. Quality, specification and cost | Reading, spec-reading exercise, cost scenario, knowledge check | 75 min |
| 4. Design, blending and sustainability | Reading, mini case, reflection, knowledge check | 60 min |
| Applied assignment | Live panel brief and a one-page yarn recommendation | 60-90 min |

Each unit ends with a short **knowledge check**. You see the right answer and a short explanation after each question, and you can retake a check as often as you like.

## Earning your certificate
Complete all four knowledge checks with a score of **70% or more**, then submit the **applied assignment** in the last section. When every graded activity is done, your **certificate of completion** appears in the last section and on your profile.

> **Tip:** Use the **Discuss** tab in the course player to ask questions and share examples from your own work. The best questions often come from a real order that went wrong.

## Key takeaways
- The course follows fibre to yarn: what yarn is, how it is spun, how it is specified and costed, and how it is designed and sourced responsibly.
- Plan about five hours across one week.
- Pass the four knowledge checks (70%) and submit the assignment to earn the certificate.
