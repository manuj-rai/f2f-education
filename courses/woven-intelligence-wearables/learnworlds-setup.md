## Course page

**How fabrics that sense, power and connect are redefining what clothing can do**

Wearable technology is moving from devices you wear to clothes that do. In this free visual course, based on Diti Shah's Fibre2Fashion article, you'll learn the four jobs textiles perform in modern wearables, see real examples from healthcare, sport, fashion and defence, and explore the conductive fibres and smart materials that make it all work. You'll also look at the challenges that still hold smart textiles back, where the technology is heading, and how to design wearables that don't become tomorrow's e-waste. Six short video lessons, a knowledge check and a free certificate.

**Who it is for:** Students, designers, merchandisers and textile professionals who want a clear, non-technical introduction to smart textiles and e-textiles.

**You will learn to:**
- Explain how wearables are shifting from devices to smart fabrics
- Describe the four functional roles of textiles in wearable technology
- Give real examples of smart textiles in four industries
- Name the key materials and techniques behind smart textiles
- Assess the challenges, future directions and sustainability of smart wearables

**Source:** this course is based on the Fibre2Fashion article [Woven Intelligence: Textiles Redefining Wearables](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/industry-article/11289/woven-intelligence-textiles-redefining-wearables) by Diti Shah (Feb 2026).

## Lessons (one Video learning activity each)

### Lesson 1: From gadgets to smart fabrics (1 min 42 s)
_Why textiles now sit at the centre of wearable technology_

**Transcript**

Welcome to Woven Intelligence. In this short course you'll see how textiles have moved from passive layers of comfort to active parts of wearable technology.
Consumers want technology that is lighter, more comfortable and part of daily life. That puts textiles, the material closest to the body, at the centre of the next wave of wearables.
Early wearables asked the body to put up with hard, bulky devices. Smart textiles flip that: the technology is built into the fabric, so it moves, stretches and breathes with the wearer.
Together, these advances turned textiles into active participants in collecting and processing data, opening the way to continuous monitoring, better comfort and more design freedom.
Keep this definition in mind. Every example in this course combines a familiar fabric with at least one of these added abilities.
We'll take these one lesson at a time. At the end you can test yourself with a short knowledge check and earn a free certificate.

### Lesson 2: Four jobs textiles do (3 min 04 s)
_Sensing, reacting, powering and connecting – built into the fabric_

**Transcript**

Smart textiles do more than hold electronics. In this lesson we look at the four functional roles they play in modern wearable technology.
Most smart garments combine two or more of these roles. Let's look at each one in turn.
A sensor built into the fabric stays in close contact with the skin all day, which is hard for a strap-on device to match.
ECG records the heart's electrical activity and EMG records muscle activity. Measuring them through fabric makes continuous tracking possible without stick-on electrodes.
For someone working outdoors or in a hazardous setting, a garment that warns about heat, UV or poor air can matter as much as one that tracks the heart.
Battery life has been one of the biggest limits on wearables. These approaches may eventually remove the need for traditional batteries, making wearables more self-sufficient and more sustainable.
Imagine adjusting your music or checking directions by touching your sleeve, without reaching into a pocket. That is connectivity built into the textile.
Put together, the four roles form a system: a comfortable textile base, sensing, power and connectivity, feeding an app or cloud service that turns raw signals into useful information.

### Lesson 3: Where smart textiles are used (2 min 31 s)
_Healthcare, sport, fashion and defence – four sectors leading adoption_

**Transcript**

Now let's see where these abilities are already making a difference. The article highlights four sectors.
Each sector values smart textiles for a different reason: care, performance, expression or safety.
When the gown or the bedding records vital signs, care teams get a continuous picture of the patient instead of occasional snapshots.
Coaches no longer have to rely only on what they can see. Garments can show how muscles are working and when an athlete is at risk of overdoing it.
Fashion brands are also exploring how to add technology subtly, without compromising comfort or looks. Interactive clothing can light up, change colour or respond to touch.
In extreme climates and high-stress missions, real-time data on each soldier's condition supports better decisions and greater endurance.
Pause the video and jot down your answer. A real use case will make the next lessons on materials and challenges more concrete.

### Lesson 4: Materials that make it work (1 min 37 s)
_The fibres, polymers and finishes behind smart textiles_

**Transcript**

The performance of any wearable textile depends heavily on material science. This lesson covers the key material innovations.
Each material solves a different problem: carrying signals, reacting to conditions, staying comfortable, or keeping the garment fresh and hygienic over long wear.
These materials can be spun, coated or blended into yarns so that the fabric itself carries electrical signals between sensors, power and connectivity.
These techniques let a fabric carry new functions while still feeling like a normal textile, which is essential if people are going to wear it every day.
However clever the electronics, a smart garment still has to behave like clothing. If it can't be washed, stretched and worn all day, it won't leave the lab.

### Lesson 5: Challenges and the road ahead (2 min 23 s)
_What still holds smart textiles back – and where they are heading_

**Transcript**

Despite strong progress, several constraints still slow adoption. After looking at them, we explore what the next generation of smart textiles could do.
Supply-chain constraints and ethical, sustainable sourcing also matter as the market grows. Solving these issues is what moves smart textiles out of the lab.
The next generation won't just add features to existing garments. It will redefine what clothing can do.
Instead of adding or removing layers, your jacket could manage your temperature for you, based on the weather and your own comfort preferences.
These capabilities could support remote patient monitoring and help reduce hospital readmissions, with continuous health insights in place of occasional check-ups.
This opens doors to dynamic fashion, performance costumes and interactive retail experiences.
The convergence of textile engineering, flexible electronics, material science and digital technology is what makes this future possible.

### Lesson 6: Sustainability and key takeaways (2 min 10 s)
_Designing smart textiles that don't become tomorrow's e-waste_

**Transcript**

Our final lesson looks at sustainability, which will increasingly decide how smart textiles are designed, made, used and disposed of.
The shift towards smart textiles can support environmental progress, but only if waste and resource use are designed out from the start.
The aim is to replace plastic-heavy components without giving up durability or performance.
Rechargeable batteries add to electronic waste. Garments that power themselves need little or no external charging, reducing battery use and energy consumption.
Design for repair matters here as much as in any other product. The garment and its electronics shouldn't have to be thrown away together.
Textiles are no longer passive layers of comfort. They are becoming dynamic systems that interact with both the body and the environment.

## Glossary

- **Smart textiles**: Fabrics embedded with sensors, conductive fibres or electronics that let garments monitor, respond and communicate.
- **Conductive fibre**: A fibre that carries electrical signals, made from materials such as silver, copper, carbon or graphene.
- **Printed electronics**: Circuits or sensors printed directly onto the surface of a fabric.
- **ECG**: Electrocardiogram: a record of the heart's electrical activity.
- **EMG**: Electromyography: a record of the electrical activity of muscles.
- **Kinetic textile**: A fabric that harvests energy from the wearer's movement.
- **Fabric supercapacitor**: A textile-based component that stores electrical charge without adding bulk.
- **Piezoelectric yarn**: A yarn that generates electricity when it is bent, stretched or pressed.
- **Thermoelectric material**: A material that turns a temperature difference, such as body heat, into electricity.
- **Shape-memory polymer**: A polymer that changes its shape or structure in response to a stimulus such as heat.
- **Microencapsulation**: Enclosing tiny amounts of a functional substance in capsules held within the fabric.
- **Electrochromic pigment**: A pigment that changes colour when an electrical signal is applied.
- **NFC**: Near-field communication: short-range wireless data exchange between devices held close together.
