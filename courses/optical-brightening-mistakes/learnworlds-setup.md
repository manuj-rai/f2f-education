## Course page

**Avoid five common mistakes that dull white fabric, waste chemicals and confuse approvals.**

A white shirt has about a second to convince a buyer, and a faint cream cast makes it look old. Optical brightening agents fix that, but they behave very differently from bleach, and familiar habits can cost whiteness and money. This free micro course, based on the Fibre2Fashion article by Waquar Arshad Khan, explains how brighteners work and walks through five common mistakes: overdosing, running hotter and longer than needed, ignoring what else is in the bath, under-heating polyester and judging white under the wrong light. You will finish with a practical routine for setting doses, controlling the bath and approving whites under D65.

**Who it is for:** Dyeing and finishing staff in process houses, quality teams and apparel buyers who specify, test or approve white fabrics and garments.

**You will learn to:**
- Explain how optical brighteners make fabric look whiter than bleaching alone.
- Find the lowest effective brightener dose with a simple dose ladder.
- Spot bath chemicals and water conditions that undo a brightener's effect.
- Put temperature control first when brightening polyester.
- Approve whites under D65 using both whiteness index and tint.

**Source:** this course is based on the Fibre2Fashion article [More Brightener, Less White: Five Optical Brightening Mistakes that Cost Process Houses Whiteness](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/industry-article/11896/more-brightener-less-white-five-optical-brightening-mistakes-that-cost-process-houses-whiteness-and-money) by Waquar Arshad Khan (Oct 2026).
