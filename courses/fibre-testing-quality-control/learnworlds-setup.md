## Course page

**Learn how fibre maturity, fineness, strength, length and moisture are tested, and what the results mean.**

Fibre properties set the limits of spinning performance and shape yarn and fabric quality, so mills test raw material before it is processed. This free reading course, based on a Fibre2Fashion article by Dr Lipsa Mohapatra, explains the core tests used in raw-material quality control, with examples drawn mainly from cotton. You will learn what maturity, cross-section, fineness, strength, elongation, length uniformity, cohesiveness and moisture regain mean, how each is measured and why it matters, then look at trash, neps, colour and stickiness. Worked examples cover maturity ratio, uniformity index and moisture regain. A short quiz checks your understanding.

**Who it is for:** Spinning mill and cotton-buying staff, quality-control trainees, and textile students who need to understand a fibre test report.

**You will learn to:**
- Explain why mills test fibre before spinning and how they use results
- Describe how cotton maturity and fineness are tested, and read micronaire correctly
- Interpret fibre strength and elongation results, including the effect of gauge length
- Calculate uniformity index and moisture regain, and explain what they show
- Explain how cohesion, trash, neps and colour affect processing and quality

**Source:** this course is based on the Fibre2Fashion article [Fibre Property Testing for Raw-Material Quality Control](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/industry-article/11878/fibre-property-testing-for-raw-material-quality-control) by Dr Lipsa Mohapatra (Oct 2026).
