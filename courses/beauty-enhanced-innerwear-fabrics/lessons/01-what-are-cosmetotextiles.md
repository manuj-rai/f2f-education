# What are cosmetotextiles?
Innerwear has long been judged on fit, comfort, support and looks. A new generation of fabrics adds another promise: a beauty or skin-related benefit. In this lesson you will learn what these fabrics are, the main ways cosmetic ingredients are built into a textile, and why the fabric itself becomes a delivery system.

## A new phase for innerwear
Innovation in innerwear has traditionally focused on how a garment performs and fits. Typical areas of development include:
- stretch and improved fit
- moisture management and breathability
- antimicrobial performance
- shaping
- temperature regulation

**Beauty-enhanced textiles** add another dimension. Instead of simply sitting against the skin, the fabric is designed to play an active role in the wearer's personal-care routine. The aim is a new generation of underwear and intimates that combine everyday apparel with elements of a skincare routine.

## Defining cosmetotextiles
These materials are known broadly as **cosmetotextiles** or **cosmetic textiles**. They incorporate cosmetic or skin-beneficial substances into fibres, yarns, fabrics or textile finishes.

Depending on the technology and the formulation, they can be designed to support:
- moisturisation
- skin softness
- freshness and fragrance
- firmness
- other cosmetic functions

Research published in the *Research Journal of Textile and Apparel* describes cosmetic textiles as textile products that can act as **delivery systems for bioactive compounds**. A bioactive compound is a substance intended to have an effect on living tissue, in this case the skin.

> **Key idea:** In a cosmetotextile, the fabric is no longer just a material that touches the skin. It is designed to become a carrier for functional cosmetic ingredients.

## Three ways to add an ingredient
There are three main routes for putting cosmetic ingredients into a textile:

| Route | How it works |
|---|---|
| In the fibre | Ingredients are incorporated while the fibre is being manufactured |
| Coatings and finishes | Ingredients are applied to the fabric as a coating or finish |
| Microcapsules | Ingredients are enclosed in microscopic capsules attached to the textile surface |

A **finish** is a treatment applied to a fabric after it has been made, to give it extra properties. Finishes are already widely used to give fabrics functions such as softness or antimicrobial performance, so the idea is familiar to textile manufacturers.

## How microencapsulation works
**Microencapsulation** is particularly significant. The active ingredient is sealed inside microscopic capsules, each with a protective outer wall, and the capsules are attached to the fabric.

This approach does two useful things:
1. It **protects** sensitive ingredients that might otherwise break down.
2. It allows the ingredient to be **released gradually** rather than all at once.

Release is triggered by everyday conditions during wear: **movement, friction, body heat and perspiration**. Research describes technologies such as microencapsulation and coating as enabling **controlled or prolonged release**, which suits a garment that is worn for hours at a time.

## What goes into the fabric
Ingredients explored in cosmetic textiles include:
- moisturising agents
- vitamins
- plant extracts
- fragrances
- antioxidants
- other cosmetic compounds

The intended benefit depends on four things working together: the **formulation**, its **concentration**, the **textile construction** and the **release mechanism**. Change any one of them and the result can change too.

This transforms the conventional understanding of fabric. A textile is usually chosen for how it looks, feels and performs. In a cosmetotextile it is also chosen, and engineered, for what it can carry to the skin. In the next lesson we look at why innerwear is such a natural place for this idea.

## Key takeaways
- Cosmetotextiles incorporate cosmetic or skin-beneficial substances into fibres, yarns, fabrics or finishes.
- Research describes them as delivery systems for bioactive compounds.
- Ingredients can be added in the fibre, through coatings or finishes, or in microcapsules.
- Microcapsules protect ingredients and release them gradually through movement, friction, body heat and perspiration.
- The intended benefit depends on formulation, concentration, textile construction and release mechanism.
