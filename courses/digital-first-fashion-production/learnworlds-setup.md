## Course page

**How try-ons, pre-orders and 3D tools could let fashion sell first and make later.**

Fashion has long made clothes first and sold them second, guided by forecasts locked in months ahead. This free course, based on the Fibre2Fashion article by Scarlett Buckley, explores whether digital tools could reverse that order. You will see why forecasting leads to unsold stock, how the EU ban on destroying unsold clothing raises the stakes, and how virtual try-ons, body scanning, pre-orders and 3D weaving could turn shopper data into production decisions. You will also learn why smaller batches do not automatically mean less clothing, and what demand-led production asks of mills and factories.

**Who it is for:** Sourcing and production teams, merchandisers, mill and factory managers, brand planners and students interested in digital, demand-led manufacturing.

**You will learn to:**
- Explain why mass fashion production depends on early forecasts.
- Describe how overproduction and returns create waste and new regulation.
- Identify digital signals that reveal demand before an order exists.
- Compare show-first, make-later models and where they fit best.
- Assess what demand-led production asks of mills and factories.

**Source:** this course is based on the Fibre2Fashion article [Could Digitalisation Change Fashion's Production Model?](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/industry-article/11917/could-digitalisation-change-fashions-production-model) by Scarlett Buckley (Nov 2026).
