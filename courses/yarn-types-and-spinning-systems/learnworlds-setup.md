## Course page

**Learn how ring, rotor, air-jet, friction, core-spun and filament yarns are made, and when to use each.**

The same cotton can become a soft, fine shirting yarn or a firm, economical denim yarn, depending on how it is spun. This free reading course draws on two Fibre2Fashion articles by Dr Lipsa Mohapatra: “Yarn Properties and Applications of Ring-Spun, Rotor-Spun, Core-Spun and Filament Yarns” and “An Overview of Open-End Spinning: Rotor, Air-Jet and Friction Spinning”. You will see how ring, rotor, air-jet and friction spinning insert twist and build different yarn structures, and how core-spun and filament yarns add strength, stretch and engineered performance. It ends with a practical guide to choosing the right yarn for each end use.

**Who it is for:** Textile and fashion students, yarn and fabric buyers, merchandisers, and spinning, weaving or knitting staff who want to compare yarn types.

**You will learn to:**
- Explain how yarn structure shapes strength, evenness, hairiness and handle
- Describe how ring spinning inserts twist and why its speed is limited
- Explain the open-end principle and compare rotor, air-jet and friction spinning
- Describe how core-spun and filament yarns are made and used
- Choose a suitable yarn type for a product and its end use

**Source:** this course is based on the Fibre2Fashion articles [Yarn Properties and Applications of Ring-Spun, Rotor-Spun, Core-Spun and Filament Yarns](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/industry-article/11905/yarn-properties-and-applications-of-ring-spun-rotor-spun-core-spun-and-filament-yarns) (Nov 2026) and [An Overview of Open-End Spinning: Rotor, Air-Jet and Friction Spinning](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/industry-article/11900/an-overview-of-open-end-spinning-rotor-air-jet-and-friction-spinning) (Oct 2026) by Dr Lipsa Mohapatra.
