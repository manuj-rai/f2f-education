# Choosing the Right Yarn Type
You have now met six kinds of yarn: ring-spun, rotor-spun, air-jet, friction-spun, core-spun and filament. This final lesson puts them side by side and gives you a practical way to choose between them for a product.

## Selection is a balance
Yarn selection is a product-specific decision. It balances:
- **Mechanical performance**, such as strength and abrasion resistance
- **Handle**
- **Appearance**
- **Processing efficiency**
- **Cost**

The fibre and the count range narrow the choice further, because each system suits certain fibres and counts. No yarn type wins on every point, which is why so many are in use.

## How the yarns are built
| Yarn | Fibre form | Structure | Feel and character |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ring-spun | Staple | Mainly helical fibres, migrating between core and surface | Soft and flexible |
| Rotor-spun | Staple | Twisted core with wrapper (belt) fibres | Firmer, bulkier, abrasion-resistant |
| Air-jet | Staple | Parallel, largely untwisted core held by wrappers at intervals | Stiffer |
| Friction-spun | Staple; DREF-3 adds a core strand | Core-sheath, uneven twist, buckled fibres | Bulky |
| Core-spun | Filament core, staple sheath | Core-sheath | Engineered through sheath and core |
| Filament | Continuous filament | Continuous filaments | Smooth; texturing adds bulk |

## How they perform
| Yarn | Strength | Typical counts | Typical uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ring-spun | High | Fine to coarse | Fine shirting, suiting, premium knitwear, hosiery |
| Rotor-spun | Usually 10–20 per cent below ring | Medium to coarse | Denim, towels, sheeting, workwear |
| Air-jet | Below ring | Medium to fine | Shirting, sheeting, knitwear, home textiles |
| Friction-spun | Weak; a core improves it | Coarse | Blankets, upholstery, filter fabrics |
| Core-spun | Depends on core and sheath | Wide range | Stretch denim, sewing threads, devoré |
| Filament | Generally high | Engineered fineness | Linings, activewear, tyre cord |

### Surface and hairiness
- **Ring-spun:** relatively hairy; compact spinning reduces this.
- **Rotor-spun:** relatively low hairiness and less tendency to pill.
- **Air-jet:** low hairiness and good pilling resistance.
- **Core-spun:** depends on the sheath.
- **Flat filament:** essentially no staple-fibre hairiness, but may snag.

### Speed and cost
- **Ring spinning** has lower productivity than the newer systems and needs extra roving and winding stages.
- **Rotor spinning** cuts out roving and separate winding, balances speed, quality and count range, and has the largest installed base.
- **Air-jet spinning** gives the highest delivery speeds of the newer systems.

> **Key idea:** Start from what the product must do, then choose the yarn whose structure delivers it at an acceptable cost.

## A simple decision path
These questions, based on what each yarn does best, point you to a starting choice:

1. **Does the product need a continuous structure, smoothness, lustre, high strength or engineered performance?** Choose **filament yarn**: flat for linings and lingerie, textured for hosiery and activewear, high-tenacity for tyre cord and ropes.
2. **Does it need the surface of a staple fibre with the strength, stability or stretch of a filament?** Choose **core-spun yarn**.
3. **Does it need fine counts, strength and softness?** Choose **ring-spun yarn**, combed or compact for premium fabrics.
4. **Does it need an economical, even, low-hairiness yarn in medium or coarse counts?** Choose **rotor-spun yarn**, which also handles short and recycled fibres.
5. **Is it polyester, viscose or a polyester-cotton blend in medium to fine counts, where delivery speed matters?** Consider **air-jet yarn**.
6. **Is it a coarse, bulky yarn, a technical core yarn or a yarn from waste fibre?** Consider **friction spinning**.

## One product, three yarns: denim
Denim shows how the choice follows the product brief rather than the product name:
- **Rotor-spun** yarn is widely used in denim, where productivity, evenness and cost matter most.
- **Ring-spun** yarn is chosen for high-quality denim, where strength and a softer handle matter more.
- **Elastane-core core-spun** yarn makes stretch denim, with the elastane giving stretch and the cotton sheath giving the familiar surface.

Sewing thread is a second example. It can be ring-spun for strength and fineness, or polyester-core core-spun when a strong filament core is needed beneath a staple-fibre surface.

## Key takeaways
- Yarn choice balances mechanical performance, handle, appearance, processing efficiency and cost, within the fibre type and count range.
- Ring yarn is the benchmark for strength and softness; rotor yarn gives economical, even yarn in medium and coarse counts.
- Air-jet gives the highest delivery speeds in medium to fine counts; friction spinning suits coarse yarns and technical core yarns.
- Core-spun yarn combines a staple surface with filament performance; filament yarn offers continuous, engineered structures.
- One product, such as denim, may use different yarn types depending on what the brief demands.
