# Digital Discovery and Media Influence
Before a garment reaches the wardrobe, it increasingly passes through a screen. This lesson looks at how online shopping and entertainment shape Dutch fashion demand, why that is a supply-chain issue as well as a marketing one, and how to avoid jumping to conclusions about media influence.

## A digitally mature market
The Dutch consumer is highly connected to digital commerce. CBS, the Dutch national statistics office, reported that in 2024:
- 80.8 per cent of Dutch people aged 12 and above had made an online purchase.
- Clothing, footwear and accessories remained the most frequently bought online category, purchased by about 59 per cent of people aged 12 and above.

In other words, for many Dutch consumers the wardrobe now starts on a screen.

## How the shopping journey has changed
The traditional journey was short and physical: **store → product → trial → purchase**.

The digital journey has more steps, most of them online: **screen → discovery → comparison → reviews → recommendation → purchase → delivery → wardrobe**.

Discovery itself is spread across many channels: social media, creators, search, marketplaces, brand websites, streaming content, recommendations, customer reviews and AI tools. A shopper may meet a product in several of these places before buying it, and may not try it on until it arrives at home.

## Why digital demand is a sourcing issue
Digital content can create demand quickly, sometimes faster than conventional retail planning can respond. When that happens, the challenge moves from the marketing team to the supply chain.

The article names five areas where businesses need more flexibility:

| Area | What it involves |
|---|---|
| Initial production quantities | Starting with smaller first orders |
| Demand sensing | Reading early signals of what is selling |
| Replenishment | Restocking proven items quickly |
| Lead times | Shortening the time from order to delivery |
| Supplier responsiveness | Partners who can react to change |

The digital consumer is therefore also a supply-chain challenge. Online behaviour, such as AI search, engagement, conversion, reviews and returns, can become **demand intelligence**: evidence that helps a business decide what to make, and how much.

> **Key idea:** When digital content creates demand faster than retail planning can respond, flexibility in quantities, lead times and suppliers becomes a competitive need.

## Media can shape taste
Fashion is increasingly seen on screens before it is bought. Streaming, music, influencers, sport and entertainment all provide visual references for how people dress and how lifestyles are represented.

The article points to the launch of *Love Is Blind: Netherlands* in September 2026 as a timely example of a global entertainment format localised for a Dutch audience. Dating formats are especially interesting for fashion research because they put clothing, appearance, identity and social impression on public display.

## But causality needs evidence
It is tempting to say, “People saw an outfit on Netflix and bought it.” Without search, sales or consumer data, that is an assumption, not a finding.

**Causality** means showing that one thing actually produces another, not just that the two appear together. The better question is whether repeated exposure to a fashion aesthetic through entertainment influences any of these steps:

1. Recognition of a style
2. Search for it
3. Purchase intention
4. Actual purchase

Each step can be measured. A brand that tracks them can tell a real media effect from a convenient story. The article’s research agenda goes one step further, asking whether digital and streaming exposure also affects how garments are used after purchase, not only whether they are bought.

### Questions to ask
- Can our online data, such as reviews and returns, inform product planning?
- Are our suppliers able to respond if a style takes off online?
- What evidence would show that a media moment really drove our sales?

## Key takeaways
- In 2024, 80.8 per cent of Dutch people aged 12 and above had shopped online, and clothing was the top category.
- The digital journey adds discovery, comparison, reviews and recommendations before purchase and delivery.
- Fast digital demand calls for flexible first quantities, demand sensing, replenishment, shorter lead times and responsive suppliers.
- Media can shape taste, but claims that a show drove sales need search, sales or consumer data.
