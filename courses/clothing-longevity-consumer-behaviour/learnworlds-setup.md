## Course page

**Why some garments are worn again and again while others stay unworn, and what brands can do.**

Many garments are bought but rarely worn. This free course, based on the Fibre2Fashion article by Nithu K, uses the Netherlands as a case study to explore why some clothes become everyday favourites while others stay in the wardrobe. Drawing on the National Wardrobe Audit 2025, you will see how comfort, fit and versatility drive repeated wear, how one consumer dresses for many occasions, and how digital shopping, media and circular-textile policy shape demand. The final lesson turns these insights into product and sourcing decisions, with lessons for suppliers in India and elsewhere.

**Who it is for:** Fashion designers, brand and product teams, sourcing professionals and sustainability students interested in consumer behaviour and longer garment use.

**You will learn to:**
- Explain why under-use matters as much as purchase volume.
- Interpret wardrobe data on why garments are loved or discarded.
- Apply situational thinking to design garments for many occasions.
- Assess how digital shopping and media influence fashion demand.
- Link consumer signals to product and sourcing decisions.

**Source:** this course is based on the Fibre2Fashion article [The Dutch Clothing Mountain: What Makes Consumers Keep Wearing Certain Clothes Again & Again?](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/industry-article/11884/the-dutch-clothing-mountain-what-makes-consumers-keep-wearing-certain-clothes-again-and-again) by Nithu K (Oct 2026).
