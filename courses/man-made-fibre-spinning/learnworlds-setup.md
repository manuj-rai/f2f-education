## Course page

**Learn how polymers become filaments, and why the spinning route shapes speed, cost and fibre properties.**

Polyester, nylon, viscose, acrylic and elastane all start as polymers that must be turned into fine filaments. This free reading course, based on a Fibre2Fashion article by Dr Lipsa Mohapatra, explains how that happens. You will follow melt spinning step by step, from drying polymer chips to quenching and drawing, and see how take-up speed gives LOY, POY and FDY. You will then learn how solution spinning handles polymers that cannot be melted, through dry, wet, dry-jet wet and gel spinning. Finally, you will compare the routes on speed, cost and solvent use, and see how each shapes the finished fibre. A short quiz checks your understanding.

**Who it is for:** Textile and fashion students, fibre and yarn buyers, and production or quality staff who want to understand how synthetic and regenerated fibres are made.

**You will learn to:**
- Explain how man-made fibres are extruded through a spinneret and solidified
- Describe the melt spinning sequence, from drying chips to drawing filaments
- Explain how drawing and take-up speed affect orientation, tenacity and elongation
- Compare dry, wet, dry-jet wet and gel spinning and their typical fibres
- Choose a spinning route from polymer behaviour and explain its effect on properties

**Source:** this course is based on the Fibre2Fashion article [Melt Spinning and Solution Spinning of Man-Made Fibres](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/industry-article/11887/melt-spinning-and-solution-spinning-of-man-made-fibres) by Dr Lipsa Mohapatra (Oct 2026).
